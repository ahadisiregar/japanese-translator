## 2. Setup

Shuffle each card deck separately and place them face down beside the board. Place the bank notes, Renovasi tokens, Paylater tokens, and Cicilan slips in a bank pile. Each player takes a token, Rp 15,000,000 in cash, and a Kewarasan tracker set to 10. Each player then draws two Self-Care cards from the Hidup Modern deck as their starting bonus. Put the two cards back into the Hidup Modern deck and reshuffle it after setup. Every player starts on GO. The player who most recently had a bad week goes first, and turn order continues to the left.

---

## 3. Object of the Game

The aim is to stay sane longer than everyone else. Each player starts with 10 Kewarasan (Sanity). Life wears it down, and when it reaches 0 you enter Burnout and are out of the game. The last player still above 0 wins. Money matters because it buys comfort and time, but money alone never eliminates a player.

---

## 4. Rules

### 4.1 Kewarasan

Kewarasan ranges from 0 to 10. Gains cannot push it above 10, and losses cannot push it below 0. You gain Kewarasan by landing on the Rest Stop (Istirahat Sejenak), by passing or landing on GO, by playing Self-Care cards, and by drawing good cards. Passing GO gives you Rp 2,000,000 and 1 Kewarasan. Landing exactly on GO gives 1 more Kewarasan on top of that. When your Kewarasan reaches 0 at any point, you are out. Your cash returns to the bank, and your properties and Renovasi tokens go to auction at half price.

### 4.2 Turn Sequence

At the start of your turn, check any Paylater tokens you hold by rolling one die for each token; a six means that token comes due. Next, lose 1 Kewarasan for each Cicilan loan you still owe. Then roll both dice and move your token that many spaces. Resolve the tile you land on by buying, paying rent, drawing a card, or paying a tax. After that, you may trade with other players and spend cash on Renovasi tokens. Finally, check your debt: if you cannot pay what you owe, follow the shortfall rule in section 4.8. Check your Kewarasan last. If it is at 0, you are out. Rolling doubles earns you a Meme card, and you roll again. Three doubles in a row sends you to Lembur Dulu.

### 4.3 Institutions

When you land on an unowned Institution, you may buy it at the listed price. If you decline, the bank auctions it, starting at half price, and the highest bidder takes it. When you land on an Institution owned by another player, you pay that owner the rent on the tile, plus any Renovasi tokens on it. Renovasi tokens may be bought on your turn. Spend Rp 500,000 to add one Renovasi token to an Institution you own. Each token adds Rp 100,000 to that Institution's rent, and a property can hold up to three tokens.

### 4.4 Transit

Each Transit tile costs Rp 2,000,000. Rent depends on how many Transit tiles the owner holds: Rp 250,000 for one, Rp 500,000 for two, Rp 750,000 for three, and Rp 1,000,000 for four. If you land on an unowned Transit tile and decline to buy it, you are stuck in traffic and lose 1 Kewarasan.

### 4.5 Utilities

Each Utility tile costs Rp 1,500,000. The PLN (Electricity) bill is a fixed Rp 400,000, and the PDAM (Water) bill is a fixed Rp 250,000. If the utility is owned, the bill is paid to the owner. If it is unowned, the bill is paid to the bank.

### 4.6 Tax, Corner Tiles, and Card Tiles

On Pajak Penghasilan (Income Tax), you pay either 10% of your cash or 2 Kewarasan, and you choose which. On Pajak Hiburan (Entertainment Tax), you pay either Rp 1,500,000 or 1 Kewarasan, and you choose which. Klinik Burnout (Burnout Clinic) has no effect when you are just visiting. If you are sent there, you skip your next two turns and lose 1 Kewarasan. Istirahat Sejenak (Rest Stop) restores 1 Kewarasan and gives no money. Lembur Dulu (Overtime) sends you straight to Klinik Burnout and costs 2 Kewarasan. Each card tile draws from the deck named on it. Resolve the card, then place it in that deck's discard pile. When a deck runs out, shuffle its discard pile to make a new one.

### 4.7 Cicilan and Paylater

On your turn, you may borrow from the bank in Rp 1,000,000 amounts, up to Rp 5,000,000 owed at one time. Each outstanding loan drains 1 Kewarasan at the start of each of your turns until you repay it. To repay, pay the principal plus Rp 200,000 interest per loan. A Paylater card gives you its cash amount now and also places a Paylater token for the same amount on your card. At the start of each of your turns, roll one die for each token; a six means that token comes due. Pay the amount. If you cannot pay, pay what you can and lose 1 Kewarasan for every Rp 500,000 still unpaid, rounded up. The token is then cleared.

### 4.8 Shortfall and Trades

Money can never go below zero and never removes you from the game by itself. If you owe more cash than you hold, first sell Renovasi tokens back to the bank at half cost, then sell Institutions and Transit or Utility deeds to the bank at half price. If you still cannot pay, the remaining shortfall becomes Kewarasan loss: lose 1 Kewarasan for every Rp 500,000 short, rounded up. You may trade cash, property deeds, and Self-Care cards with other players at any time on your turn. Kewarasan can never be traded.

### 4.9 Movement Words on Cards

Skip 1 turn or Skip 2 turns means you do nothing on the next turn or turns you would have taken, and the player after you continues normally. Move to next Institution means move forward to the next Institution tile, and you resolve it. Move to any Institution means you choose any Institution tile and move there.

### 4.10 Winning

The last player with Kewarasan above 0 wins. If the final players all reach 0 on the same turn, the one with the most cash wins. If the game reaches 90 minutes, the player with the highest Kewarasan wins. A tie is broken by the highest cash.

## 7. Balance Notes

Every card's Kewarasan swing falls between -3 and +2. In this edition, the actual range is -2 to +2, and the largest gains are the Self-Care cards, which give +2. Kewarasan is capped at 10, so players who are healthy early cannot bank extra buffer. Transit and Institution rent can snowball, so Renovasi tokens are capped at three per property and Transit rent flattens at four tiles. Political cards make up 30 of 160 cards, about 19%, so the political theme is present without taking over each game. Kewarasan is the only elimination mechanic, and money loss converts into Kewarasan loss once cash runs out. This keeps money from ending a game on its own.

## 8. Playtest Checklist

1. Does one player snowball in the first 20 minutes, and does the rest of the table have a way back?
2. Is any tile never landed on in five test games? If so, adjust its position or effect.
3. Are the political cards too one-sided? Read them aloud to players from different viewpoints and ask whether each side feels mocked.
4. Does Kewarasan drop so fast that the game ends before 45 minutes?
5. Does Kewarasan stall so long that the game passes 90 minutes?
6. Do Cicilan and Paylater feel like real pressure, or like a fine that players forget about?
7. Do players understand the shortfall rule without a rules lookup?
8. Does the Self-Care gain feel rewarding, or does it feel like a token gesture?
9. Are any cards repeated in a single game? Shuffle and track draws for accuracy.
10. Do players laugh at the same cards? A card that gets silence from every table should be rewritten or cut.

## 9. Content Note

The political cards target systems, incentives, and institutions, including bureaucracy, budgets, subsidies, lobbying, elections, and hypocrisy. They do not name living politicians, celebrities, or private individuals. Institutions appear only as types, such as a bank, a platform, or a credit rating agency, and the satire relies on publicly known business models and public policies. No card invents crimes or scandals and presents them as fact. Dark jokes deal with mortality, debt, and anxiety but do not glamorize self-harm, suicide, eating disorders, or drug use, and none involve minors. Burnout and anxiety are satirized, not promoted. There are no slurs, and no card attacks anyone for their race, religion, ethnicity, gender, sexuality, disability, or nationality. Satire is aimed upward at systems and incentives, not downward at people for who they are. The content guardrails in the design brief are met.
